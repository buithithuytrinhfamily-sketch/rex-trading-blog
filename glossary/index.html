Forex glossary

65 plain-language definitions. Search by term or browse alphabetically.

65 terms

Ask

The price at which a dealer offers to sell the base asset to you.

ATR

Average True Range, a measure of recent ranges that includes gaps relative to the prior close.

Backtest

A historical simulation of a rule, sensitive to data quality, costs and implementation assumptions.

Base currency

The first currency in a pair, whose units are being priced.

Basis risk

Risk that a hedge and the exposure it is intended to offset move differently.

Bid

The price at which a dealer offers to buy the base asset from you.

Breakout

A move beyond a predefined price boundary under a stated rule.

Candlestick

A display of open, high, low and close over one period.

Carry trade

An exposure seeking a financing advantage while retaining exchange-rate and other risks.

Central bank

An institution responsible for monetary functions defined by its mandate and jurisdiction.

CFD

A contract for difference referencing price changes rather than necessarily delivering the underlying asset.

Correlation

A measure of co-movement over a stated dataset; it can change and does not prove causation.

Counterparty risk

The risk that the other party fails to meet its obligations.

CPI

Consumer Price Index, a measure of price changes for a defined consumer basket under an agency’s methodology.

Currency cross

A currency pair that does not include the US dollar.

Divergence

A mismatch between a price extreme and a corresponding indicator extreme.

Diversification

Spreading exposures so outcomes are not wholly driven by the same risks.

Drawdown

The decline from a prior equity peak to a later value, usually expressed as a percentage.

DXY

A specific US dollar index with a defined currency basket; it is not every possible measure of dollar strength.

Economic calendar

A schedule of releases and events, usually with time, expected values and importance labels.

Equity

Account balance adjusted for open-position profit or loss and applicable charges.

Expectancy

Average expected outcome per trade under a model, including winning and losing outcomes and costs.

False breakout

A move beyond a boundary that fails to continue according to the strategy’s definition.

Fibonacci retracement

Proportional levels drawn between chosen swing endpoints; their usefulness requires testing.

Free margin

Equity minus margin in use, subject to a platform’s definitions.

Futures

Standardized exchange-traded contracts with specified terms, including expiration or settlement rules.

GDP

Gross Domestic Product, a measure of production within an economy during a specified period.

Hedge

An offsetting exposure intended to reduce a specified risk, potentially adding cost or basis risk.

Heikin Ashi

A transformed candle display whose values are not necessarily executable market prices.

Leverage

The ratio of exposure to the capital supporting it.

Limit order

An order that restricts the execution price but may never fill.

Liquidity

The ability to trade size with limited delay and price impact under prevailing conditions.

Look-ahead bias

Using information in a historical decision that was not available at that time.

Lot

A contract-size convention; a standard forex lot commonly represents 100,000 base units, while other products differ.

MACD

An indicator built from differences between exponential moving averages and a signal line.

Margin

Collateral required to support a position; it is not the maximum possible loss.

Margin call

A notification or condition related to insufficient margin under the provider’s rules.

Market order

An order seeking execution at available prices rather than guaranteeing a price.

Moving average

A rolling calculation that smooths historical observations.

NFP

Nonfarm payroll employment, part of the US employment report; revisions and other components also matter.

Notional value

The amount of market exposure represented by a position.

Out-of-sample

Data kept separate from rule selection to test how a method generalizes.

Overfitting

Adapting a model so closely to a sample that it captures noise rather than a stable relationship.

Pip

A conventional quote increment, often 0.0001 for non-JPY pairs and 0.01 for JPY pairs; check the instrument.

Pipette

A fraction of a pip, commonly one tenth.

Pivot point

A calculated reference level derived from a prior period’s prices, not a guaranteed turning point.

Quote currency

The second currency in a pair, used to express the price of the base currency.

R multiple

An outcome divided by the original planned cash risk of that trade.

Range

A price interval or a market condition described by repeated movement between boundaries.

Real yield

A yield adjusted for an inflation measure or expectation; the exact definition matters.

Resistance

A defined price area where prior behavior suggests supply or reduced buying; it can fail.

Risk-on / risk-off

Shorthand for broad risk appetite; assets can disagree and the label is not a trade signal.

Risk-reward ratio

A comparison of planned loss and planned gain; the ratio alone does not establish an edge.

RSI

A momentum calculation comparing recent gains and losses; extreme values do not guarantee reversal.

Slippage

The difference between an expected or requested execution price and the actual fill.

Spread

The difference between the current ask and bid; one part of transaction cost.

Stop out

Forced position reduction or liquidation when a margin threshold is breached.

Stop-limit order

A stop trigger followed by a limit order; execution may fail if the market passes the limit.

Stop-loss order

An instruction intended to exit at a trigger; ordinary stops do not guarantee the final execution price.

Support

A defined price area where prior behavior suggests demand or reduced selling; it can fail.

Swap

A financing adjustment associated with holding certain retail forex or CFD positions overnight.

Tick size

The smallest permitted price increment for a particular instrument.

Tick value

The cash value of one tick for a specified contract size, converted when needed.

Trend

A directional pattern measured over a specified timeframe and definition.

Volatility

The variability of price changes over a stated window and sampling method.
